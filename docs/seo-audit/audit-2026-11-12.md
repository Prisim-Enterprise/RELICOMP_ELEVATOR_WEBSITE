with the brand suffix duplicated, eight meta descriptions over 160, two pages with no
canonical/og:image/JSON-LD, a static `robots.txt` silently shadowing the dynamic route, and
live body copy referring readers to three guides that did not exist.

## SEO — 9/10

**Working.** Every mechanical signal is clean across all 31 pages: unique titles under 60
characters with the brand applied exactly once by the root layout, descriptions within the
display limit, one descriptive H1 per page, self-referencing canonicals matching sitemap
form, og:image everywhere, zero images without alt text, no legacy keywords tag. `robots.txt`
is now actually served from `app/robots.ts` with `Host` and `Sitemap` declared — it never had
been, because a static `public/robots.txt` was taking precedence. Placeholder pages dropped
from 11 to 1, and the one remaining is `noindex, follow` and excluded from the sitemap.
Structured data covers Product, Service, FAQPage, LocalBusiness, BlogPosting, WebPage, HowTo
and BreadcrumbList with no parse errors.

**What holds it at 9:**

1. **No pricing published anywhere.** Zero currency figures across the entire site, including
   the price guide. `pricesApproved` is `false`, so Product schema correctly emits no `offers`
   node. But the cost cluster is the largest and easiest opportunity in your own keyword
   research — *home elevator lift cost* (6,600/SD 14), *home lift price* (5,400/SD 13),
   *cost of home lift in Bangalore* (1,900/SD 13). The guides explain what drives cost
   honestly and well, but a competitor who publishes a range will take the snippet and the
   comparison visit. This is a commercial decision, not a technical gap.
2. **Content coverage is incomplete against your own plan.** Four live pages sit well below
   the word-count targets in `relicomp-keyword-map-word-counts.md`:

   | Page | Words | Target |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | `/industries/commercial-offices` | 408 | 800–1,000 |
   | `/industries/apartments-residential` | 488 | 800–1,000 |
   | `/services/lift-modernization` | 551 | 700–900 |
   | `/services/custom-elevator-manufacturing` | 634 | 800–1,000 |

3. **Nine planned pages do not exist.** `/industries/hospitals` (hospital elevator 320/SD 22,
   hospital elevator size 170/SD 13, stretcher lift dimension 70/SD 10 — all winnable),
   `/industries/hotels-restaurants`, `/industries/warehouses-industrial`, and six of eight
   location pages.
4. **Review markup is self-serving.** Google discounts ratings a business publishes about
   itself, so the AggregateRating will not produce SERP stars on its own. Your Google
   Business Profile is the only route to those.

## GEO — 8/10

**Working.** The entity graph is properly built: 31 schema types cross-referenced by `@id`
rather than duplicated, so engines resolve one entity. Organization carries a sized logo,
full PostalAddress, areaServed for both states, and two typed ContactPoints declaring four
languages (en, kn, hi, ta). LocalBusiness carries verified coordinates resolved from your
own Google Business Profile, `openingHoursSpecification` and `hasMap`. The 4.5/5 from 530+
reviews is machine-readable as AggregateRating with seven attributed Review nodes, generated
from the same object that renders the visible strip so the two cannot drift. External
authority is real and checkable: IS 14665, NBC 2016 Part 8 Section 5, and the Karnataka
Lifts, Escalators and Passenger Conveyors Act 2012. Full HTML including all JSON-LD is
delivered without JavaScript. No directive blocks GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, CCBot
or Google-Extended. `llms.txt` now maps all six guides with descriptions — it previously had
no blog section at all, so the most citable pages on the site were invisible to the file
that exists to advertise them.

Two September findings are closed. The **brand-entity split is gone**: no email address is
published anywhere on the site or in schema, so the unreachable `relicompelevators.in`
domain is no longer advertised (this was resolved in the codebase before this pass, by
removing the address). And the **fabricated credential issuers are gone** — `/about`
previously attributed certifications to "ISO Certification Body" and "National Safety
Authority", neither of which exists, and ISO certifies no one directly. Those claims now
state the published standards the equipment is built to.

**What holds it at 8:**

1. **No named humans. This is still the single largest gap, and it is unchanged.**
   `src/content/team.ts` is an empty array, `TeamSection` renders nothing, and
   `buildTeamPersonJsonLd` emits nothing. The seven Person nodes in the graph are review
   authors, not staff. Elevators are safety-critical, high-value equipment; AI engines and
   Google's E-E-A-T assessment both look for identifiable people behind expertise claims,
   and there are none. Fabricating them would be far worse, so the architecture correctly
   publishes nothing — but the point stays unearned until real names and consent arrive.
2. **No original data.** Still no proprietary benchmark, published range or research an
   engine would prefer to cite over a competitor. This is downstream of the pricing
   decision: the six guides are strong synthesis, but synthesis is what every competitor
   also has. A published range, an average project timeline, or installation data from your
   600+ jobs would be genuinely citable.
3. **No verifiable certificate.** Standards compliance is an honest claim but a weaker one
   than a certificate number from a named accredited body.
4. **No published email.** Removing the `.in` address closed the entity split, but phone and
   WhatsApp are now the only channels. A business with no published email address is a
   marginally weaker trust and accessibility signal.

## AEO — 9/10

**Working.** 221 question-phrased headings, up from 87. 134 FAQ Q&A pairs in FAQPage schema
across 29 of 31 pages. 18 real HTML comparison tables, every one with proper `thead` markup,
up from 2 — covering MRL vs MR vs hydraulic, traction vs hydraulic, AMC plan tiers and the
situation-to-service routing table. An 8-step HowTo generated from the shared
`installationSteps` array, so it cannot diverge from the visible timeline.
SpeakableSpecification on 13 pages, up from 6, now including all six product pages.
`/contact` carries ContactPage, LocalBusiness with geo and hours, WebPage and — new — a
FAQPage; it was the only live page with no FAQ schema. FAQ answers resolve the question in
the opening sentence at 35–60 words.

The September contradiction is closed: body copy advertising 24/7 breakdown support while
schema published Mon–Sat office hours now states explicitly that these are two different
services, from a single shared constant rendered on `/contact`, the `/services` hub and as
an FAQ on the AMC page. "Are they open now?" is answerable without a reader inferring it.

**What holds it at 9:**

1. **Cost questions still have no numeric answer.** The September audit put it exactly:
   "without a numeric range it will still lose the snippet to a competitor who publishes
   one." The guides now answer *what drives* the cost, which is a real improvement over
   deflecting to an unpublished page, but the snippet for "home lift price in Bangalore"
   goes to whoever prints a figure.
2. **Local intent coverage is still two cities.** The homepage names Hassan, Udupi, Hospete,
   Kushalnagar, Chamarajanagara and Dindigul; only Bangalore and Mysore have a page to land
   on. "Elevator company in Hassan" has nothing to answer with.
3. **FAQ answers are not in the server-rendered DOM.** Radix mounts accordion content only
   when a visitor opens an item, so the `.faq-answer` elements referenced by
   SpeakableSpecification do not exist in raw HTML until then. Every answer is published in
   full in the FAQPage JSON-LD on the same page, which is what search and answer engines
   consume, so the practical impact is small. Forcing the text into the HTML costs the
   expand/collapse animation site-wide; that trade was tested and rejected, and the
   reasoning is recorded in `FAQSection.tsx`.

wrong value is gone.
